Domain routing against the earlier bandits

Every method picks one of four models per question and sees only that model's saved answer. The page puts the two new domain-aware policies, the FT confidence-bound variants, Greedy and ε-greedy on one cost–accuracy plane, next to the best routing that full knowledge of the answers would allow.

τ = 0.85, 10,000 rounds
$1.24 vs $2.12

Domain TS (κ = 50) holds accuracy at 0.849 for $1.24 per 1,000 questions. FT KL-UCB, the only earlier method that reaches 0.85, overshoots to 0.870 and pays $2.12.

What the domain prior adds
−18% to −20%

With the same λ mechanism, the domain prior cuts the cost at τ = 0.85 from $1.56 to $1.28 (κ = 20) or $1.24 (κ = 50) over 10,000 rounds. Over 3,000 rounds the saving is about 5%, at slightly lower accuracy.

Greedy and ε-greedy
89.05% at $3.09

The best Greedy-type mean stays below fixed Gemini 3.8 Flash (89.63% at $2.91). Full knowledge per domain would buy 89.05% for $2.14.

Still open
≈ 1.9× the optimum

At τ = 0.85 the per-domain optimum costs $0.67. At τ = 0.89 the domain policies stop at 0.879–0.883, below FT KL-UCB.

The methods

FT-UCB familyPaper Algorithm 6: each round, take the cheapest model whose confidence bound is at least τ. Variants change only the bound: original Hoeffding, tight (0.5 ln t), Bernoulli KL (c = 0 or 3) and LCB.
KL-UCB per domainOne independent FT KL-UCB per subject, each held to the same τ. This asks every subject to reach τ, which the overall goal does not require.
Domain UCB (algorithm 1)Per question, the model with the lowest normalized cost − λ × upper bound. A Beta prior centres each model's subject accuracy on its pooled accuracy with a weight of κ questions. λ grows with the cumulative shortfall below τ.
Domain TS (algorithm 2)Same prior and λ, but the accuracy is a Thompson draw: first the model's pooled accuracy, then its subject accuracy.
No-domain ablationThe same two policies with κ = ∞, so every subject shares one estimate per model. The gap to the domain versions is the value of the subject label.
Greedy, ε-greedyWarm-up calls, then the model with the highest estimated accuracy, with uniform exploration at rate ε. Rows are the best of 98 and 328 tested settings.

Read with care

  • The FT and domain policies use each model's mean cost per call. The Greedy reports use the charge of each call, with 9 waived charges counted as zero. Both come from the same CSV, and Gemini costs $2.91 per 1,000 questions either way.
  • κ, η = 1 and β = 0.5 for the domain policies, and the best Greedy settings, were chosen on these same questions. The ε-greedy fresh-order check drops its best-below-Gemini setting from 89.05% to 88.63%.
  • The optimum lines use every saved answer of every model, which no online router sees. They are lower bounds on cost, not targets a router can reach.
  • The SDs measure sensitivity to question order on one fixed question set. They are not confidence intervals for new questions.
